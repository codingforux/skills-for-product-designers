# Interview ticket

The answer is in someone's experience. A **coworker** (sales, support, a PM) sees patterns across many customers, second-hand. A **customer** has it first-hand. One ticket can cover several conversations. It takes two sessions, days apart, and stays claimed in between.

## Plan

1. **Ask the user**, in one round: who they will talk to (coworker or customer, which role, how many), how they will reach them, and how long each conversation is.
2. **Write the discussion guide** to pass **the Mom Test**: ask what the person did, not what they think of your idea. "Tell me about the last time you…" beats "Would you use…". Keep every question open, so it doesn't suggest an answer.
   - **Intro**: who the user is, why they're asking, whether it's recorded, and that notes are anonymized.
   - **Warm-up**: their role and a typical week.
   - **Three to five topics**, each tied to the ticket's question, with an opener and follow-ups ("What happened next?", "Can you show me?").
   - **Close**: "What should I have asked?" and "Who else should I talk to?"
   - For coworkers, add what customers ask for and complain about most. For several customers, add a short screener.
3. **Post the guide** as a comment on the ticket. Tell the user to run `/discovery` on the map with their notes after the conversations.

## Synthesize

1. **Read the notes or transcript** the user brings, pasted or as a path outside the repo.
2. **Post a synthesis comment**: the participants as P1, P2 and on, each with role and context; the findings, each with its quotes; surprises; persona changes to propose at wrap-up; follow-ups. Keep what people **did** apart from what they say they would do. Strip every quote of names, companies, places and anything else that identifies someone.
3. **Resolve** when the user says the conversations are done: the finding answers the ticket's question across all of them. Follow-ups become tickets.

Done when every topic in the guide is accounted for and every quote is anonymized.
